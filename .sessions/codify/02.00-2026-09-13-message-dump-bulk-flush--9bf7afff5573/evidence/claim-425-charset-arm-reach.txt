================ 4. WHAT THE LANDED CODE IS ENTITLED TO DEPEND ON ==================
Entitled (each half is evidence on file, not inference):
  (a) At messages.py:162-166's exact spelling — `content_type=` given, NO charset declared anywhere,
      `.charset` is None, `.text` therefore applies `or "utf-8"`, and StringPayload always encoded
      utf-8 — the str round trip HOLDS.  That is the sub-case claim-425's own verdict names as holding,
      and it is separately certified by the published CL:aiohttp/web-response-str-body-text-property
      (Verdict: HOLDS, row 421) and by CL:aiohttp/web-response-str-body-accessor-surface (422's
      enumeration naming `.text` the only accessor returning the string's content).
  (b) `.text` at that site is TOTAL: `_body` is a StringPayload whose `_value` is exactly
      s.encode("utf-8"), and `s.encode("utf-8").decode("utf-8") == s` for every str the decode path
      in _error_body can return (strict decode, or errors="replace" -> U+FFFD).  So the read cannot
      raise on any upstream payload.  Section 3 shows it True for ASCII, non-ASCII and replacement text.
  (c) The value is not lost on the wire either (CL:aiohttp/web-response-str-body-wire-bytes, HOLDS).
NOT entitled:
  (d) No general "a str body round-trips through web.Response" premise.  claim-425 refutes the
      universal, and the two published HOLDS records carry the mismatch in their own Actual: caveats.
  (e) The hold at (a) rests on TWO independent defaults agreeing — StringPayload's hardcoded utf-8
      (payload.py:430-458, `real_encoding = "utf-8"`, the setter passing no encoding/content_type) and
      Response.text's `self.charset or "utf-8"` (web_response.py:723-730) — with nothing in the
      library reconciling them.  aiohttp's stable documentation promises the opposite rule
      ("tries to encode the given data based on Content-Type HTTP header"); the installed source never
      implements it.  A later change that names a charset at that constructor, or that reads `.body`
      again, breaks the probe silently for non-ASCII payloads and loudly (UnicodeDecodeError, outside
      any handler arm) for a non-decodable one — while the documentation would tell the author it is safe.
      That is the latent trap this pass records; see QB:assumption-ledger/str-body-charset-doc-divergence.
